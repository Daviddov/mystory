# Writing like Dr. Seuss: a working checklist

Condensed from the research the user shared (Downloads\לספר כמו דוקטור סוס.txt). Not imitation of his
books: use the mechanisms, in Hebrew, with original characters.

## The core rule
Let the child learn the game fast, then surprise them again and again inside the game they learned.
Every page needs something the child can predict, and something they did not expect (pattern + violation).

## Checklist for the text
1. **Problem on page 1.** No introduction. The conflict is small and clear ("I don't want to go to kindergarten").
2. **Rhythm first.** Read every line aloud. Lines of similar length and beat; it should feel like a song.
3. **Rhyme that creates anticipation.** End the line on the rhyming word, so the child can guess it.
   Hebrew endings that rhyme easily: ־ת/־בת (יושבת/ארנבת), ־ה (גבה/טובה), ־ום (מקום/היום), ־וף (בסוף/פרצוף).
4. **A refrain** the child can shout: identical every time (e.g. "לא בא לי ללכת לגן של הארנבת!").
   Give the other side a refrain too (Mom's "אולי מחר.").
5. **Repetition with a change:** A → A → A' → A → big change. Same frame, a new detail each time.
6. **Escalation:** small → strange → absurd → peak → resolution. Each request bigger and sillier than the last
   (stay home → come to work → a wagon with a bell and a horn → a hundred stars and a thousand dots).
7. **Words as toys:** sound words (פְּרִי-פְּרִי, קראנץ' קראנץ'), funny comparisons (קטנה כמו גרגר של שום),
   triples (שתקה... ושתקה... ושתקה...).
8. **Simple vocabulary, rich structure.** Few, short words; the cleverness is in the pattern.
9. **Chaos, then order.** The child enjoys the rule-breaking, but the story lands safely.
10. **No preaching.** No moral sentence. The ending shows; the child discovers. A cheeky ending is fine
    if the user wants it; a small twist in the picture can carry the warmth.
11. **Short pages:** 2–5 lines per page, 10–14 pages, for ages 3–5.

## Checklist for the pictures
- Iconic characters: simple silhouette, 1–2 signature props, same clothes per setting on every page.
- The picture adds a joke the text does not say (a storm cloud only over her head; one eye on the candy jar).
- Daydreams and wishes go in a big cloud-shaped thought bubble.
- One clear focus per page; exaggerated, energetic poses; warm and safe, never scary.
- No text or letters in the picture (the app shows the text).

## Before importing, read the whole story aloud once
Does each page have something to predict and something to surprise? Does the refrain come back at least 3 times?
Does the last page echo the first?
